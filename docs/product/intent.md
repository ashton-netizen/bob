# What BOB is for

Ash's own answers, taken in a three-round interview on 29 October 2026. This file is the top of BOB's brief. Everything else in the brief, the product plan and the code serves what is written here. Plain words throughout. Where a line says "Ash said", it is his answer, not Claude's reading.

## The one-line purpose

BOB lets a first-time builder take an idea from a sentence to a working thing by talking and clicking, with a map that shows where they are and lights up as each stage is proven. The first-time builder is Mags. Ash is the method owner.

## How BOB gets built (Ash said)

BOB builds BOB. The rebuild runs as a BOB project, through BOB's own gates, places and approach, so that building BOB is itself a BOB experience. Where a part of BOB is not finished yet, Claude plays that part by hand and says so each time. That is allowed. What is not allowed is building BOB some other way and bolting the experience on at the end.

So: the first real BOB session is this one, Ash and BOB building BOB. Mags's first session comes second, on a small real idea of her own.

## The moment that is "that's BOB"

The building of BOB feels like BOB: one question at a time in plain words, a map that lights up, three approvals by button, and no one ever asked to type, paste or hunt for anything. When Mags then takes one idea from a sentence to a working thing the same way, with the map fully lit at the end, BOB is done.

## The picture (Ash's correction, 29 October 2026, later the same morning: the product first, not the progress)

"The trick around BOB is: I can see what I've built." The picture at the heart of BOB is a flowchart of the product being built: what starts it, what comes in, the sources and databases it reads, what it does, what it decides, what it produces and what it shows. One box per part, arrows for how information moves. A box lights when that part is built and proven. It is a picture of the thing, not a scoreboard.

Ash edits the picture in his own tool: BOB writes it as a mind map file he opens in MindNode and changes freely, and BOB reads his changes back into the brief and redraws.

The ten-stage mind map stays, behind the picture, as one thin strip lit as each stage is proven: progress only, never the headline.

1. Define the outcome and scope
2. Establish what good looks like
3. Map the workflow and ecosystem
4. Establish data and source foundations
5. Design the experience
6. Choose architecture, models and skills
7. Prepare the build environment
8. Build in complete, testable increments
9. Verify quality and release
10. Operate, measure and learn

Nothing on the picture is a gate, a rule, a test or a code. The first working look of 29 October (the ten stages in front, the product as a list) was not this, and was corrected the same morning.

## Where BOB lives (Ash chose: pane in Claude Code plus a browser map)

The conversation and the buttons sit in the pane inside the Claude desktop app's Code tab. The map and the brief open as a browser page, delivered as a clickable link that Claude has looked at first. This reuses what is already built. A standalone web app is not the plan.

## Mags's first five minutes (Ash chose: one question, map behind it)

BOB asks one question in plain words: what do you want to build? The unlit ten-stage map sits behind the question. Her answer lights stage one and BOB asks the next question. No project name to type, no command, no folder to pick. Claude does all of that.

## Approvals (Ash chose: three, by the person building)

Three approvals per project, each by a button in the pane:

1. The brief
2. A working look
3. The finish

The person building the project approves their own project. On Mags's projects, Ash sees a three-line note each time. On BOB's own build, Ash approves. A click on Approve starts the work; an approval never lands on an idle window.

## Never stuck (Ash chose: only approvals and outward actions stop it)

BOB pauses only at the three approvals and before anything irreversible or outward: a send, a publish, a spend. Everything else it does itself and reports in three lines (where it is, what is live, the next step with its doer). A round never ends on a next step whose doer is Claude.

If a person-only click is unavoidable (a restart, a picker the app owns), BOB shows one button with one plain-words line saying what it buys, and logs the ask as a BOB bug to design away.

## Step Change thinking inside BOB (Ash chose: build-brief and reviewers inside, the rest on call)

Built into BOB's stages: the build-brief ten blocks, the Infinite Leverage reviewers, and the Step Change brand through scm-design-skill. Pulled in only when a stage's decision needs them: agile strategy, the virtual board, creative territories, the IP Log. BOB never forces the whole toolkit onto a project.

## What survives from the current code (Ash chose: keep the gates, rebuild the face)

Keep: the approval gate (no edit without an approval), the evidence gate (no round ends without proof), the records, the tests, the Approve-starts-work rule.

Rebuild from the ten-stage map, in plain words: the pane, the map page, the status page, and every word a person reads.

## The cure that comes first (Ash chose: never asked to act with no reason or button)

BOB never asks a person to type, paste or hunt for anything. Any ask is one button with one plain-words line, or it is a BOB bug. The other three cures follow: every round ends by showing the map, not describing it; no engineer-speak on any page a person reads; no round ends on a step Claude could take.

## What this file does not decide

The exact look of the pane and the map (a working look is the second approval). The order in which the ten stages get their schematic detail. The date of Mags's first session. These are settled in the brief and the rounds, under BOB.
